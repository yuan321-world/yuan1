## 阅读材料

### Making Room for Quiet on Campus

At one university, finding a seat was not always the hardest part of studying. Finding the right kind of seat was. The library offered large tables for group projects, while the student centre provided comfortable chairs near a busy cafe. Yet students who needed an hour of uninterrupted reading often moved from place to place. They did not necessarily want a completely silent campus. They wanted to know where quiet could be expected.

A student committee first proposed putting quiet signs on every floor of the library. After talking to other students, however, its members changed their approach. Some students explained that discussing a difficult problem aloud was an important part of their work. Rather than restrict these conversations everywhere, the committee asked the university to open an unused room beside a small courtyard. It would be a quiet study room, with ordinary desks and no special equipment.

The university agreed to a six-week trial. Students could enter without booking, but they were asked to take calls outside and avoid saving seats for absent friends. Instead of appointing someone to watch the room all day, the committee placed a short explanation of the shared rules at the entrance. Volunteers visited twice a day to collect comments and check whether the room was being used as intended.

By the end of the trial, more students were using the room than in the first week. Comments suggested that knowing what to expect made it easier to settle down to work. The committee called this a modest beginning, rather than proof of a dramatic improvement in learning. Exams were approaching, and wet weather might also have brought more people indoors. The trial had measured visits and collected opinions, not tested whether students remembered more of what they read.

The committee recommended keeping the room open and checking its use again during a less busy month. Its members also wanted group-work areas to remain available. Quiet, they concluded, was one useful choice among several. A campus could support independent reading without treating conversation as the enemy of learning. The aim was to make different kinds of study easier to arrange, not to declare one method best for everyone.

## 题目

### 1. Why did the committee change its original plan for the library?

题型：细节理解

- A. The library could not afford to provide more quiet signs.
- B. Some students needed places where they could discuss their work.
- C. The courtyard already had enough seats for all library users.
- D. Most students preferred studying in the cafe to using the library.

### 2. The word "modest" in paragraph 4 most nearly means _____.

题型：词义猜测

- A. unexpected and difficult to explain
- B. complete and unlikely to change
- C. unsuccessful and not worth continuing
- D. limited in scale rather than dramatic

### 3. What can be inferred from the committee's discussion of the trial results?

题型：推断

- A. The approaching exams had no influence on use of the room.
- B. Students' comments were enough to prove that their memory improved.
- C. Increased visits alone did not establish that learning had improved.
- D. Wet weather was shown to be the main cause of the increase in visits.

### 4. What is the author's attitude towards providing quiet study spaces?

题型：作者态度

- A. Supportive, while recognising the value of other study settings.
- B. Enthusiastic, because silence is the best condition for every student.
- C. Doubtful, because quiet rooms cannot serve any useful purpose.
- D. Indifferent, since arranging study spaces is of little importance.

### 5. What is the main message of the passage?

题型：主旨概括

- A. Universities should replace group-work areas with silent reading rooms.
- B. Providing different study settings can meet different learning needs.
- C. Expensive equipment is essential to the success of a new study space.
- D. Counting visits is the best way to measure how much students learn.

## 答案与解析

答案：**1 B · 2 D · 3 C · 4 A · 5 B**

### 第1题

答案：B。第2段说明，部分学生认为出声讨论难题是学习的重要部分，因此委员会没有到处限制交谈，而是另设安静房间。原文依据：Some students explained that discussing a difficult problem aloud was an important part of their work. A的标牌经费问题未提及；C把庭院旁的闲置房间偷换为庭院已有充足座位；D把部分空间的描述扩大为多数学生的偏好。

### 第2题

答案：D。modest在此修饰beginning，后接rather than proof of a dramatic improvement in learning，表示这是规模有限、尚不能夸大学习效果的开端。原文依据：The committee called this a modest beginning, rather than proof of a dramatic improvement in learning. A的意外或难以解释、B的彻底稳定、C的失败及不值得继续均不符合上下文；第5段建议继续开放也排除C。

### 第3题

答案：C。第4段只报告到访次数和意见，未测试记忆效果，并提出临近考试、下雨等可能影响使用量的因素，因此无法仅凭到访增加就断定学习提高。原文依据：The trial had measured visits and collected opinions, not tested whether students remembered more of what they read. A把可能有影响说成完全无影响；B把主观意见当作记忆改善的证明；D把might所表达的可能原因升级为已证实的主要原因。

### 第4题

答案：A。全文呈现安静空间的价值，同时强调讨论及小组学习仍应得到支持，作者态度是支持而不绝对化。原文依据（第5段）：A campus could support independent reading without treating conversation as the enemy of learning. B把一种有用选择说成对所有人最佳；C否认安静空间作用；D声称空间安排不重要，均与全文不符。

### 第5题

答案：B。第1段提出不同空间需求，第2—4段叙述试行和评价，第5段概括应为不同学习方式提供选择。原文依据：The aim was to make different kinds of study easier to arrange, not to declare one method best for everyone. A与保留group-work areas矛盾；C与no special equipment矛盾；D与第4段对到访量证据局限的说明矛盾。
